Web platform development

buddy

AI HYROX training partner

A training platform that helps two athletes prepare for a HYROX doubles race together, even when they live in different cities. It predicts each partner's and the team's finish time, splits every station between them, and builds a weekly plan that adapts to what they actually do.

The goal: My friend and I signed up for HYROX Madrid as a doubles team, but we train in different places and had no shared view of our progress. buddy pulls in WHOOP recovery, sleep and workouts, keeps raw data private and shares only what each partner chooses. It predicts our times run by run and station by station, suggests who does what down to the metres per turn, and gives every day a session, macros per meal and a log. I designed and built it with Claude Code, Claude and Gemini.

Web platform development

rookie

AI career assistant for students

An AI-powered platform that helps students manage their internship and job applications. Students paste a job link, get an honest score out of 10 and a tailored CV and cover letter, and track every application in one clear board.

The goal: During application season, students apply to dozens of roles at once. They lose track of deadlines, interview dates and replies across tabs, spreadsheets and inboxes, and often send the same generic CV everywhere. rookie reads each job listing, explains why you fit and what the catch is, and writes a one-page CV and cover letter for that role in eight languages. It also reads company replies in Gmail to update each application's status on its own. Built for the IE EdTech Challenge with Claude Code and Gemini.

iOS app & landing page

FreelanceFlow

Tax. Sorted.

An iOS finance and tax tracker for European freelancers. It covers six countries (DE, ES, UK, FR, NL, IT) and calculates tax estimates using each country's actual progressive brackets, allowances and social contributions. It also generates invoice PDFs, sends reminders before quarterly deadlines and exports a CSV for accountants. Privacy-first: no accounts, no servers, no tracking. Freemium, with Pro at €4.99/month.

The goal: Most freelance finance apps are built for the US (LLCs, the IRS, dollars), which leaves European freelancers guessing how much to set aside for taxes. I wanted to give them a simple, trustworthy answer to "What do I actually owe, and when?" without handing their financial data to a cloud service. I built the SwiftUI app and its marketing site with AI-assisted development.

Brand identity

Marmalade

Coffee & Kitchen

A complete visual identity for a coffee shop and kitchen: a serif wordmark and an emblem logo with a jar icon, an earthy palette (taupe, espresso, charcoal, cream) and a moodboard for the space. It also covers applications: loyalty stamp cards, takeaway cups in several colourways and metallic foil options for the logo. Calm, warm and premium, a café that feels crafted rather than trendy.

The goal: To give a local café an identity that feels like a place you want to stay in, not just grab a coffee from. Every touchpoint, from the cup to the loyalty card, needed to feel consistent and recognisable, so the brand builds loyalty and stands out from generic coffee chains.

Palette

  • Ivory Linen#F9F7F4
  • Pewter#9C9791
  • Espresso Bean#201C15
  • Cacao#3E2C16
  • Cappuccino#7E7261

Key decisions

  • One quiet wordmark. A light, widely spaced serif that works on every colour in the palette, tested under both "Coffee and Home" and "Coffee and Kitchen".
  • An emblem for stamping. The oval jar badge (est. 2025) carries the brand where the wordmark is too long: stamp cards, cup lids, the back of a card.
  • Loyalty as a ritual. Ten-stamp cards in ivory and cacao, so collecting feels like part of the café rather than a coupon.
  • A cup system, not one cup. Six colourways mixing wordmark, M monogram and emblem, so the brand stays recognisable across every order.
  • Finish matters in premium. I tested the logo in 20 metallic gold tones on charcoal, from Vegas gold to champagne gold and ecru, before choosing the foil.

Presentation design & visual storytelling

Underqualified Intern

A 10-slide visual story about how I got my marketing internship at MVC Videra without the experience the role asked for. The deck follows three attempts, from a failed first approach to a cold WhatsApp message and a strategy document that led to a signed offer. Three attempts, five months

  1. Oct 2025 · Attempt 1Failure. My only lead was a passing mention of a marketing role for German speakers in the IE DACH community newsletter. I messaged the number cold. The role was for a five-year specialist; I was a second-year student.
  2. Attempt 2Partial success. Instead of accepting the no, I followed up and proposed myself as an intern, with no job listing. Not an application, a proposal. The reply was kind but vague, and I almost gave up.
  3. Nov 18 - Dec 31, 2025 · Attempt 3The test. Weeks later they asked for a call and B2B work samples. I had none, said so, and sent what was mine: a coffee shop business plan, a student reselling platform, websites and decks. The call went well, and the bar went up: create content from two real case studies, with no brief.
  4. Dec 31, 2025Doing more than asked. Rather than samples, I wrote a 10-page B2B Marketing & Content Creation Strategy over Christmas, after asking my IE marketing professor for feedback. He called it "quite remarkable".
  5. Jan - Feb 2026The wait. Silence, then "you're our first option", then paperwork. I kept checking in, quietly and professionally, and told no one.
  6. Mar 2026Signed. A six-month internship offer. When I met the team in Madrid, a five-minute introduction with the CRO turned into thirty-five once he heard I study Computer Science & AI alongside business.

What each trait looked like

  • Initiative. A cold WhatsApp to a stranger with no referral, then 10 pages when I was asked for samples. Initiative isn't one moment, it's a posture.
  • Resilience. Mine isn't loud, it's quiet and delayed. I sit with rejection, process it privately, and then I come back.
  • Resourcefulness. Using work that wasn't B2B as proof I could do B2B, and finding someone who already knew the answer and asking directly.
  • Comfort with uncertainty. Weeks of silence after submitting the strategy. The only option was to keep going as if the outcome might still be good.
"A no is only final if you accept it as one."

About me

I learn ten times more by building than by studying for exams.

Too yellow for consulting?

I came to university to study design. Two weeks in, I switched to Computer Science & AI without ever having written a line of code, and now study it alongside Business Administration. The creative side never left. At an IBM event in Madrid this year, a personality quiz put me firmly at the "yellow" end of the scale: creative, big ideas. I half-jokingly asked whether that made me too yellow for consulting. It wasn't entirely a joke. What I've found since is that I don't want to choose. The work I care about most sits where strategy, design and technology meet, and is built around people rather than just the technology.

How I learn

I join group projects on purpose where I have to own the hard technical parts and teach myself, with AI as my pair programmer. That is how Connectr+, FreelanceFlow, buddy, rookie and RUEDA came to exist. At Medudy, publishing a single course meant hours of copying titles, links and video IDs between tools by hand. Nobody asked me to fix it, and I had never used an automation tool. So I mapped every step, taught myself n8n and connected the pieces with Claude. Today a course goes live from a link and a few keywords with about 90% less effort. Medudy and ProfiL taught me the same thing: the model is rarely the hard part. Choosing the right problem and bringing people with you is.

Asking people to take a chance on me

I got my marketing internship at MVC Videra through a cold message in my second year, a step most students take in their fourth, without the background the role asked for. Three months in, they asked me to stay on. I was also selected for IEX Labs' [YOU] on AI + X programme from around 200 applicants. So far, every time someone has taken a chance on me, it has paid off for both sides.

Not knowing how, yet

At 15 I spent an exchange year in New Zealand. Before university I spent several months travelling and working on my own across Australia and Southeast Asia, including on a cattle station in the Outback. It taught me that not knowing how to do something yet is never a reason not to try.

Off the screen

I grew up with three older siblings and have played field hockey for more than ten years, so I know when to lead and when to step back and pass. I'm a certified personal trainer, train hybrid and for HYROX, box, and teach art classes for girls from disadvantaged backgrounds in Berlin.

What's next

AI is changing the job market, and the jobs themselves, faster than anyone expected. I want to be in the middle of that: learning from teams that already do it well, then one day building my own company, most likely in health and wellness.
